Our stars

    Stars you catch land here. Check them off during the trip.

    What kind of place this is. Crestone is a town of about 100 people at 8,000 feet, home to more than two dozen spiritual centers, and a certified International Dark Sky Community. There are a handful of restaurants, one brewery, and a food co-op. Three hot springs and Great Sand Dunes National Park are 30 to 45 minutes away. November is quiet and cold, with clear days. Items marked book need a reservation; check means call the week before, since small-town hours move.

    7:45 am

    Morning at the stupa and the Ziggurat

    The spiritual centers sit on the hillside south of town in the Baca Grande. Morning light hits the Sangres right behind them, and it is quiet at this hour. An easy way to start the day: about an hour of walking, with time for the grocery run after.

    • Tashi Gomang Stupa. White Tibetan stupa with prayer flags and a view across the whole valley, open to the public every day. Walk around it clockwise, which is the custom at Buddhist stupas.

      About the stupaDirections

    • The Crestone Ziggurat. A stone spiral tower on a rocky hill at the southeast edge of the Baca Grande, built as a private place for prayer and open to the public. Walk the spiral ramp to the top for a 360 degree view. Visitors leave small rock arrangements at the base as an intention, a nice thing to do together on an anniversary.

      About the ZigguratMap

    Dress warm and speak quietly. These are active places of practice. Allow about an hour for both.

    Soak at Joyful Journey Hot Springs booked dark sky

    Thirty minutes west in Moffat, so leave Raven's Haven by 3:45. 90 minutes, $65, towels included, with access to the private pool and the public pools. Sunset is just before 5, right in the middle of the soak: the sky goes orange over the valley and the Sangres glow pink, then the first stars come out before you leave.

    • Ease in at The Tower Pool (104°F) after the massages.
    • Be in the private pool for sunset, just the two of you.
    • Finish in The View Pool (108°F), the hottest, facing the mountains. It is whisper-only.
    • The cool pool (98°F), two hot tubs, a cold plunge, and a sauna are there for breaks.
    Good to know: no alcohol in the pool area, so save the wine for the cabin. The sauna is included, and there is a cold shower by the pools. The front desk sells snacks and drinks. Robes rent for $5.

    10 am

    Hike: Great Sand Dunes National Park

    Forty-five minutes south. Park at the main Dunes lot and walk out onto the sand. The creek bed will be dry in November, so you can cross straight onto the dunes.

    • High Dune: about 2.5 miles round trip and 688 feet of climbing in sand, with no trail. Allow two to three hours. The view from the top covers the whole dune field.
    • Or Mosca Pass Trail: a forest trail up a creek valley, 3.5 miles one way. Walk the first mile or two and turn back. A good pick if sand sounds like too much.

    Picnic on the sand at noon. Entrance fee is $25 per vehicle.

    Park hiking guideDunes parking map

    12:45 pm

    Hike: Zapata Falls

    Ten minutes south of the park entrance. A rough 3.5 mile gravel road up to the trailhead (any car with care, AWD ideal), then a half mile walk to a 25 foot waterfall tucked inside a rock crevice. In November it is partly frozen into ice columns. The overlook on the way up has the best single view of the dunes anywhere.

    Good to know: the last stretch means stepping into the creek and over slick rocks. Wear waterproof boots, and in ice, stop at the mouth of the crevice and look in.

    3:45 pm to 5:45 pm

    Sunset at the Greenhouse, Sand Dunes Recreation book

    In Hooper, about 25 minutes north of Alamosa and on the way back toward Crestone. A 21 and over hot springs inside a 10,000 square foot greenhouse: pools at different temperatures, a sauna, a steam room, tropical plants, and the Steel Box Bar. This is the anniversary soak, timed for sunset just before 5: the light through the glass goes gold, and the Sangres to the east turn pink. Two hours, a drink each, no phones. Call 719-378-2807 to confirm Greenhouse hours and whether they take reservations.

    What to get: a drink from the Steel Box Bar, where the house beer has been brewed with hops grown in the greenhouse itself. One visitor wrote up the seared tuna as the food pick. For the pools, the long zero-entry pool with the fountain is the comfortable one (around 98°F). The smallest tubs run 107 to 110°F, so go easy in those.

    7 pm

    Anniversary steak dinner at the cabin

    About 50 minutes home from Hooper. Open the good bottle while the pan heats.

    • Steaks: out of the fridge 30 minutes early, salt well. Cast iron very hot, about 4 minutes a side for medium rare, then baste with butter, garlic, and rosemary for the last minute. Rest 5 minutes.
    • Potatoes: start them in the oven at 425°F first, about 40 minutes, so they finish with the steaks.
    • Salad on the side.

    Water boils at a lower temperature at 8,000 feet, so anything boiled takes longer. Searing and roasting work the same.

    8:30 pm

    Fire, the good bottle, and the gifts. Then outside: lay the blanket down, lie back, and watch for shooting stars. The Taurid meteor shower peaks in early November and throws slow, bright fireballs. Read each other the letters written before the trip.

    7:45 am

    Light hike: Willow Lake Trail after check-out

    The trail into the peaks you see from the cabin. Check out first and bring the packed car, since the trailhead is just a few minutes from town. Drive east on E Galena Ave until it turns to gravel and ends at a large lot with a sign. The road is rough but fine for a mid-size car.

    The full trail climbs steadily for about 4.4 miles to Willow Lake and its 100 foot waterfall, about 6 hours round trip, so today is a taste: walk up for about 45 minutes, turn around, and be back at the car by 9:15. The views of the valley open up behind you as you climb.

    Good to know: it climbs the whole way and starts high, so go slow. Expect snow and ice on the trail in November. Bring water, layers, and something with grip. If it is icy at the start, walk the Baca Grande stupa and Ziggurat loop again instead.
