Our stars

    Stars you catch land here. Check them off during the trip.

    What kind of place this is. Crestone is a town of about 100 people at 8,000 feet, home to more than two dozen spiritual centers, and a certified International Dark Sky Community. There are a handful of restaurants, one brewery, and a food co-op. Three hot springs and Great Sand Dunes National Park are 30 to 45 minutes away. November is quiet and cold, with clear days. Items marked book need a reservation; check means call the week before, since small-town hours move.

    Thursday, November 5

    Arrival night · in by 9 pm · nothing in Crestone serves food that late
    On the way in

    Groceries before the last 12 miles

    Crestone has a small co-op (Elephant Cloud Market) but it closes early. Stock up in the last real town on your route: Safeway or Walmart in Alamosa if flying into ALS, City Market in Salida if coming from the north, Walmart in Alamosa or Safeway in Salida if driving from Denver or Colorado Springs. Pick up: wine, cheese, bread, fruit, chocolate, eggs and bacon for Saturday, and firewood if the cabin does not supply it.

    Drive times: Alamosa 1 hr 15 min · Colorado Springs 2 hr 45 min · Denver 3 hr 45 min. The last stretch from Moffat to Crestone is 12.5 miles of straight paved road with no lights. Watch for deer and elk.

    10 pm

    First night dark sky

    Fire on, unpack, pour a glass, then go outside with a blanket for fifteen minutes. The moon is a thin waning crescent that rises after midnight, so the Milky Way will be overhead with no interference. This is one of the darkest places in the lower 48.

    Friday, November 6

    Crestone day · the town, the temples, a couples massage, a soak
    9 am

    Breakfast at The Cloud Station check

    Breakfast burritos and coffee in a converted gas station on South Cottonwood. Backup: Bayou Mountain at Bob's Diner on Galena (New Orleans style, weekday mornings).

    10:30 am

    Temple drive through the Baca Grande

    The spiritual centers sit on the hillside south of town along Camino Baca Grande. Three stops, each a short walk from the car:

    • Tashi Gomang Stupa. White Tibetan stupa with prayer flags and a view across the whole valley. Walk the circle together.
    • Haidakhandi Universal Ashram. Hindu temple with a quiet garden. Shoes off at the door. Visitors welcome.
    • The Crestone Ziggurat. A stone spiral tower on a hill. Short climb to the top, 360 degree view of the Sangres and the valley.

    Dress warm and speak quietly at all three. They are active places of practice.

    12:30 pm

    Lunch at Our Food Is Art check

    Tacos, bowls, and smoothies on Galena. Open Thursday to Saturday. Backup: Crestone Brewing Co. on Silver Avenue.

    2 pm

    Couples massage at Crestone Massage Therapy book

    Jane Adorney and Steve Fillenberg work out of their off-grid strawbale home 10 minutes from town, with more than 50 years of experience between them. Book both for the same slot so Rebecca and Rafael are on the tables at the same time. Ask for 60 or 90 minutes and hot stone if they offer it. 720-512-1802.

    Backup: Joyful Journey has on-site therapists, so a massage can be added before the soak at 5 pm. Smiling Heart Healing Arts and Jenny Che Raven are solo therapists in town.

    5 pm

    Evening soak at Joyful Journey Hot Springs book

    Thirty minutes west in Moffat. Three mineral pools at different temperatures, two hot tubs, a cold plunge, and a dry sauna, all facing the mountains. Day use runs until 8 pm on Fridays. Call ahead to confirm day passes are available: 719-256-4328.

    7:30 pm

    Dinner at The Bliss book

    Thai and French in the Bliss Inn on Silver Avenue, the one place in town open seven days a week. Reserve a table and ask for one by the window.

    Backup: Sage's Hearth or People's Pub & Kitchen, both in town. Call for hours.

    Saturday, November 7

    Anniversary day · the dunes, a waterfall, an adults-only hot springs, and a long night at the cabin
    8:30 am

    Breakfast at the cabin

    Cook together. Pack the picnic for lunch while the coffee brews: sandwiches, fruit, a thermos of something hot, a blanket.

    10 am

    Great Sand Dunes National Park

    Forty-five minutes south. Park at the main Dunes lot and walk out onto the sand. The creek bed will be dry in November, so you can cross straight onto the dunes. Climb High Dune if the legs are willing (about 2.5 miles round trip, a real workout in sand), or just pick a ridge and sit. The dunes are empty in November and the light on the Sangres behind them is the whole point.

    Entrance fee is $25 per vehicle. The visitor center has a short film worth ten minutes.

    12:30 pm

    Picnic at the dunes, then Zapata Falls

    Eat on the sand with the dunes in front of you. Then drive 10 minutes south to the Zapata Falls turnoff: a rough 3.5 mile gravel road up to the trailhead (any car with care, AWD ideal), then a half mile walk to a 25 foot waterfall tucked inside a rock crevice. In November it is partly frozen into ice columns. The overlook on the way up has the best single view of the dunes anywhere.

    3 pm

    The Greenhouse at Sand Dunes Recreation book

    In Hooper, 35 minutes west of the park and on the way back toward Crestone. The Greenhouse is a 21 and over hot springs built inside a 10,000 square foot greenhouse: four pools at different temperatures, a sauna, tropical plants, and a bar that serves you in the water. This is the anniversary soak. Two hours, a drink each, no phones. Call 719-378-2807 to confirm Greenhouse hours and whether they take reservations for the day.

    6 pm

    Anniversary dinner book

    Option A, eat there: the Mile Deep Grille at Sand Dunes Recreation serves to the pool deck, so you can go straight from the water to a table. Easy, warm, no driving between.

    Option B, back in Crestone: The Bliss again, or Our Food Is Art if you liked it Friday (Saturday close is 8 pm, so reserve for 6:30 and leave Hooper by 5:30).

    8:30 pm

    Cabin night dark sky

    Fire, the good bottle you saved, and the gifts. Then outside: lay the blanket down, lie back, and watch for shooting stars. The Taurid meteor shower peaks in early November and throws slow, bright fireballs. Read each other the letters written before the trip.

    Sunday, November 8

    Departure · leave Crestone by 2:30 pm for Alamosa, by 1 pm for Colorado Springs, by noon for Denver
    9 am

    Slow breakfast in town

    The Cloud Station or the cabin. Walk the two blocks of downtown one more time. Stop at the Crestone Mercantile and the co-op for a small thing to take home.

    11 am

    Valley View Hot Springs book

    Forty minutes north near Villa Grove, on a hillside with the whole valley below. Natural rock pools fed by warm springs, a waterfall pool, and a sauna, no chemicals, no lights, no phones allowed near the water. It is clothing optional and run by a land trust, so it feels like nowhere else. Reservations are required and non-members can only book one day ahead, so call 719-256-4315 on Saturday morning. Day use opens at 11 am.

    If that is not your thing: hike the North Crestone Creek Trail instead. Trailhead is 2 miles north of town past the campground. Walk along the creek through aspen and pine for a 3 to 4 mile out and back, then an early lunch at Crestone Brewing Co.

    1:30 pm

    Lunch on the way out

    Heading to Alamosa: Milagros Coffee House or San Luis Valley Brewing downtown. Heading north to Salida: The Fritz or Amicas. Then the airport, or the long road home.

    Pack

    • Real winter layers. Highs 40s, lows single digits to teens. Wind on the dunes.
    • Waterproof boots with grip, hat, gloves, sunglasses
    • Two swimsuits each so one is always dry, plus sandals for pool decks
    • Headlamp or flashlight with a red mode for stargazing
    • A blanket you do not mind getting sandy
    • The letters, written before the trip

    Altitude and hot water

    • Crestone is at 8,000 feet. Drink more water than feels necessary.
    • Hot springs plus altitude plus alcohol hits hard. Pace it.
    • Sunscreen even when it is cold, especially on the sand
